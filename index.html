a better way to run india

the citizen's techocracy

Public money should be traceable to the person who paid it, every decision should carry the name of whoever made it, and public office should be reachable on ability. None of this is a new idea. It is only now cheap enough to actually do.

what this is — and what it is not

winning elections and running a country
are two very different skills.

Winning an election calls for mass campaigning, fundraising, media dominance, and coalition arithmetic. Running a country calls for policy depth, long-horizon planning, and disciplined execution. The two sets of skills overlap only partially, and that gap is the root of the problem.

This system narrows the gap from both ends. It makes reaching office cheaper and cleaner, so that capable people can arrive there without a fortune behind them — and it makes everything they then do with public money visible, questionable, and attributable to a name.

it is not

computers ruling over people
a machine deciding your life
a cure for every problem
removing elections or parliament

it is

every rupee visible to every citizen
every middleman named & answerable
a fair, affordable path to office
your voice in decisions, all year round

three rules behind everything

principle 01

people decide; technology helps

No machine ever takes the decision. Technology's only job is to show the facts, name its sources, and explain what each choice will cost — before you vote, not after things go wrong.

principle 02

built to survive bad leaders

Every rule here assumes that one day, a dishonest person will hold power. The system is designed so that even then, they cannot quietly loot it or wreck it.

principle 03

jobs by ability, support by need

Jobs and positions go to those who can do them. Help and support go to those who need them. The two are never mixed up — which is what keeps both fair.

what the state owes its members

every citizen is a shareholder
in the enterprise called india.

Every budget line, every tariff, every appointment flows into the shared profitability and the shared morality of that enterprise. The framing instils two things Indian public life chronically lacks: ownership over public money, and the expectation of returns — measurable outcomes, published accounts, answerable management.

The comparison has a limit, and it is worth stating plainly. A company can ignore anyone who holds no shares. A country cannot ignore children, the disabled, or the outvoted. So the state is built as three things at once.

a firm

The country has to earn: industry, competition, trade, ambition. Growth is not the point in itself. It is what there is to distribute.

an insurance pool

Lost work and illness are temporary conditions, not verdicts. Those unable to contribute for a time are carried, and matched to work within their capability. No one is entitled to perform surgery — but no one is written off either.

a trust

Some citizens will never be able to earn, through age or disability. They receive a guaranteed stipend floor by virtue of membership in the nation, protected even in downturns.

The floor is fixed and indexed to prices; only spending above it flexes with prosperity. Without that rule the state falls into what economists call the pro-cyclical trap — cutting hardest on the most vulnerable at the exact moment they are least able to bear it.

the triguna model of the state

a person is a composition of
tamas, rajas and sattva.
so is a country.

Inertia, ambition and clarity. A nation carries a profitability responsibility and a moral responsibility at the same time, and it moves forward only when the two are held in proportion.

guna 01

tamas — the ballast

courts, due process, the slowness of amendment

Tamas is usually maligned, but in statecraft it has a protective face: constitutional rigidity, due process, the deliberate difficulty of changing the founding rules. This is the ballast that stops a panicked majority or a popular strongman from burning down in one year what took fifty to build.

guna 02

rajas — the growth engine

industry, competition, trade, ambition

Rajas is the state's capacity to generate. The taxation reforms, the export logic, and the allocation of roles by merit all live here. A country with no rajas has nothing to distribute, and redistribution becomes an argument over a shrinking quantity.

guna 03

sattva — the clarity

education, transparency, justice, the dignity floor

Sattva is the state's clarity. Nearly everything this system ranks as highest priority is sattvic, and that ranking is the whole argument: this is not a greed machine but a structure in which clarity governs ambition rather than the reverse.

the proportion

no guna may dominate.
none may be starved to zero.

The right proportion between the three is not a constant that can be computed. It is the living output of the democratic process itself. The system does not choose the balance, and it is not built to. Its job is narrower and harder: to make sure the process sees clearly what it is choosing.
