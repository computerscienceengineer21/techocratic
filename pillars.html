03 — the 7 pillars

seven pillars hold the house.

Seven mechanisms hold the system honest in the present, while education does the slower and deeper work: making integrity and clear judgment the cultural baseline rather than the exception.

scroll
01
pillar 1 — schools first

good schools first, always

Education here does not mean degrees or job quotas. It means the capacity for clear judgment. Every other pillar holds the line in the meantime; this is the one that changes what has to be held.

  • schools get first claim on the country's money — before everything else
  • protected by hard rules, so no government can cut it for short-term applause
  • laws change behaviour in years; schools change a country's character in a generation
02
pillar 2 — breathing room

taxes that let business breathe

Excessive extraction chokes demand. Blanket protection sets off a longer chain: superior goods become unaffordable, so demand for them dies; with no demand, no one builds the factory. Protection without conditions does not build industry — it embalms its absence.

  • help for an industry always comes with an expiry date and clear targets
  • meet the targets and the support is no longer needed; miss them and it ends on schedule regardless
  • this is what South Korea did right — and what India before 1991 got wrong
03
pillar 3 — ask anything

ask the government anything

Any question about public money — "how much did my district spend on roads last year?" — returns an answer that cites the actual government documents, line by line. Right to information stops being a request you file and becomes infrastructure that answers.

  • no source, no answer — the system is never allowed to guess
  • a live chart shows where the country's money is actually going
  • even secrets have rules: every secret file is logged, reviewed, and opens automatically after a fixed time
  • this is built first — because no one can honestly campaign against it
04
pillar 4 — the ladder

jobs by ability, help by need

This follows what the proposal calls the channel-separation principle: support for disadvantaged groups moves out of the channel that allocates roles — admissions, jobs — and into the channel that allocates resources — school funding, coaching, stipends. No one is denied a role they earned, and no one is handed a role they cannot perform.

  • quotas reduce automatically as school results between communities become equal
  • a published formula anyone can check — not a political football
  • first make education equal, then judge by ability alone — in that order
05
pillar 5 — quick justice

justice in months, not decades

The problem with our courts is not soft judges — it is that punishment arrives ten years late. And late punishment teaches everyone that breaking the rules is safe.

  • more judges, strict time limits on trials, digital evidence, published judgments
  • fast and fair — never punishing one person harshly just to "send a message"
  • anyone who threatens a judge, inspector or whistleblower faces these same fast courts
  • and only a court decides what counts as a "threat" — never the officials themselves
06
pillar 6 — the glass house

well-paid, fully open government jobs

Every official's income and property, public. But openness alone is not enough. Pay officials badly and you do not filter for virtue — you filter for those with money coming from somewhere else. Economists call this adverse selection, and it produces the exact opposite of the intent.

  • salary as good as a private company would pay
  • every financial detail open to the public — a condition of entry, not a penalty
  • and betrayal of that trust brings real, career-ending punishment — the formula that worked in Singapore
07
pillar 7 — the sunlight

no secret money in politics

No more electoral bonds — the instrument that let donors fund parties anonymously, with the identity of the payer hidden from the voters it was meant to inform. If someone is paying a politician, the public has a right to know who, and how much.

  • every donation disclosed, capped, and published the moment it happens
  • for any leader, a standing public record of who funded them and what those funders subsequently received
  • money in politics cannot be banned — but it can be dragged into the sunlight, where voters can judge it

Schools matter most — but open books come first. One is the destination; the other is the road.

why "ask anything" is built first

It needs no new law and nobody's permission — only public documents and working software. And it creates what everything else depends on: clean, open records that anyone can check.
