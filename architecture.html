02 — how it works

four simple steps.

Every big decision about your money follows the same path: people say what matters most, hard rules protect the basics, a computer works out the options and shows its working — and people always get the final word.

scroll
step 01

people say what matters most

More money for schools? For farmers? For defence? You vote on the priorities — from your phone, with your identity verified. Too busy to follow every topic? Hand your vote on that topic to someone you trust, and take it back whenever you like.

step 02

some things can never be cut

Money for schools, fair courts, and the minimum support for the poorest are protected by hard rules — like a fixed deposit no one can break in a moment of panic. Changing these rules is possible, but it takes years and a very large majority. A generation can change them; a mood cannot.

step 03

a computer shows the options — and its working

Once people set the priorities, a public computer works out the possible budgets and what each one would lead to. Like a good maths student, it shows every step of its working — and says honestly when it is not sure. Change its assumptions yourself, and watch the numbers move.

step 04

people always get the final word

The computer only suggests — it never decides. A machine can answer "what is the fastest way to reach this goal?" but never "what should our goals be?" A machine that picks goals for people would just be a new kind of priest — and this system bans that by design.

and watching everything

an AI that asks the hard questions

Before any big decision, an AI checks: What could go wrong later? Has this idea failed somewhere before? Does it break the protected rules? Its only power is to ask these questions loudly and publicly — it can never give orders.
